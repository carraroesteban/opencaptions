What students get

  • The lecture, as it's said, on their own phone or laptop, in the original or translated into their language.
  • The slide, in their language. When the lecturer's computer shares the presentation, each new slide appears on the phone with its text translated.
  • Any word explained. Tapping a term in the captions explains it in plain words, from where the lecturer used it.
  • "What did I miss?" for anyone who arrives late or loses the thread.
  • The transcript after class, searchable, downloadable, and a box to ask every lecture of the course a question, answered with the class and the minute.

For the accessibility office and IT

  • Runs on your own computers. A laptop in the lecture hall, or one server for the whole campus. Transcripts stay there.
  • Private when it must be: on a Mac with macOS 26, speech recognition and translation run on the Mac itself, and no audio leaves the room.
  • Company sign-in (OpenID Connect) for staff, and a crew role for student assistants who only start and stop rooms.
  • Every page checked against WCAG 2.2 AA, in light and dark.

Automatic captions make mistakes, especially with names and formulas. Add the course's terms to the glossary, and where a student's accommodation requires verbatim accuracy, a human captioner is still the standard.

What it costs

The software is free and open source. In the cloud, about US$ 0.54 for each hour a room is speaking, plus US$ 0.40–0.60 per translated language, and nothing while it's silent; on a Mac, free per hour. See how to caption an event.
