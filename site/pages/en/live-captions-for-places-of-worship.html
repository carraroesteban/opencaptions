What the congregation gets

  • Captions on the screen next to the hymn lyrics, large and readable from the back.
  • Captions on their phone in the language they read, from a QR code on a card or in the bulletin. No app.
  • A translated voice in headphones, when you turn it on, for people who would rather listen than read.
  • The room's own sound in hearing aids and earbuds paired with the phone, alongside any hearing loop you already have.
  • Each service written down to read again, search or share.

For the volunteers who run it

  • One computer with a cable from the sound desk, or one phone near the pulpit for a small room. It pauses itself in silence and during music, and picks up again when someone speaks.
  • A glossary for names, places and the words of your tradition, so they're spelled right.
  • Free on a Mac: with macOS 26, speech recognition and translation run on the Mac itself, with no account and nothing leaving the building.

What it costs

The software is free. On a Mac, nothing per hour. With Google's Gemini in the cloud, about US$ 0.54 for each hour of speech plus US$ 0.40–0.60 per translated language; the translated voice in headphones costs more, about US$ 2.20 an hour. See how to caption an event.
