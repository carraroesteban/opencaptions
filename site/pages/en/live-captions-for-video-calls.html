Who it's for

  • People who are deaf or hard of hearing, in calls, classes and videos that don't have captions, or whose captions aren't good enough.
  • Anyone listening in their second language: read the call in your own language while people speak, then reread the parts you missed.
  • Students and remote workers who want a searchable transcript of what they heard.

How it works

  1. Download OpenCaptions (free, for Mac and Windows) and open it. On the welcome screen, choose Just for me.
  2. Choose what to listen to: the computer's sound (a Zoom, Teams or Meet call, a YouTube video, a web class) the microphone (you, or the conversation around you), or both, to caption both sides of a call (with headphones).
  3. Choose a language to translate into, or keep the original, and press Start captions.

Captions appear a couple of seconds after each word, in big text. Floating captions opens a small window that stays on top of everything, so you can keep it over the call or the video.

Which sound it can caption

  • Windows: everything the computer plays, from any app. In the browser's sharing window, choose the entire screen and turn on "Share system audio".
  • Mac: the sound of a browser tab (Chrome or Edge): Google Meet, Teams or Zoom in the browser, YouTube, an online class. Apple doesn't let browsers share the whole computer's sound, so desktop apps can't be captioned this way yet.
  • Any computer: the microphone.

Private by design

OpenCaptions runs on your own computer. In this mode nothing is shared, not even with other devices on your Wi-Fi. The transcripts stay on your computer, and you can delete any of them for good. The AI is Google's Gemini (paste a free key once) or, if nothing should leave the computer, open models that run on it.

What it costs

The app is free and open source. With Gemini, the free tier is enough to try it; with a paid key, an hour of speech costs about US$2, and silence is never billed. With the models on your computer, it costs nothing at all.
