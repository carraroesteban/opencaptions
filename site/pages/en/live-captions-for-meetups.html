The smallest setup that works

  1. Run OpenCaptions on a laptop (the Mac app or the Windows launcher), give the room a name and pick the languages.
  2. Turn on the public address in one click, so phones on any network can reach it.
  3. Open the room's audio page on a phone and set it near the speaker. The same screen shows the QR code for the audience, keeps the phone awake and, on Android, warns before the battery runs out.
  4. Everyone scans the QR code and reads along in their language. Afterwards, the transcript is there for the people who couldn't come.

What it costs

Nothing, on a Mac with macOS 26: speech recognition and translation run on the Mac, with no account. With Google's Gemini (its free quota is enough for a few meetups), about US$ 0.54 an hour of speech plus US$ 0.40–0.60 per translated language once you pay. You can try it live on your phone first.

Good to know

  • A phone's microphone works best close to the speaker; a cheap lapel mic plugged into the phone is better still.
  • Add the names of speakers, products and acronyms to the glossary before the talk.
  • Captions are automatic and make mistakes: say so on the screen, as OpenCaptions does by default.
