Who we are

OpenCaptions is a free, open-source project, published at github.com/carraroesteban/opencaptions. We don't sell anything, and we have no accounts, newsletters or forms. To contact us, open an issue on GitHub, or use GitHub's private reporting for anything sensitive.

This website

  • No cookies. If you switch between light and dark mode, your choice is saved in your own browser (local storage) and never sent anywhere.
  • Nothing from other companies. Fonts, images and videos are served by this website itself. There are no ads, trackers, social media buttons or embedded videos.
  • No visitor statistics. We don't count or follow visitors.
  • Anonymous visitor statistics. We count visits with GoatCounter, which uses no cookies and keeps no personal data: the page, the site you came from, your browser and screen size, and your country, which is worked out from your connection and then discarded. We use it only to know which pages help people.
  • Hosting. The website is hosted on GitHub Pages, and the downloads on GitHub. GitHub keeps technical logs (such as your IP address) to run and protect its service, under the GitHub privacy statement.

The OpenCaptions software

OpenCaptions runs on a computer or server chosen by whoever organizes the event. It has no telemetry: it never sends us usage data, transcripts, audio or anything else. We don't see who uses it.

When it runs, it connects only to the services its operator sets up: the AI that makes the captions (Google's Gemini, or a model on a computer at the venue), and optionally Cloudflare for a public web address and a messaging service for alerts. Those services process data under their own terms.

If you attend an event that uses OpenCaptions

The organizer is responsible for the captions, the transcripts and your data, not the OpenCaptions project. Captions pages set no cookies and don't ask who you are. Questions about how an event handles speech, transcripts or recordings should go to its organizer.

Organizers can find what to tell speakers and the audience, with a ready-made notice, in Licenses and responsibilities.

Your rights

We don't hold personal data about visitors or users, so there's nothing for us to give you, correct or delete. For data that GitHub keeps, see GitHub's privacy statement. If anything here is unclear, ask us on GitHub.
