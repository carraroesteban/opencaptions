Why now, in the United States

The Department of Justice's 2024 rule under Title II of the ADA requires state and local governments' websites and apps, including the meetings they stream there, to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA. That level includes success criterion 1.2.4, Captions (Live): live audio in a stream needs captions. In April 2026 the department moved the compliance dates to April 26, 2027 for governments serving 50,000 people or more and April 26, 2028 for smaller ones and special districts, without changing what is required (the rule, the extension). This page is not legal advice: ask your counsel what applies to you.

Automatic captions are not perfect. Where every word must be exact (a hearing, a vote that will be quoted), have someone review the transcript, or book a human captioner.

What residents get

  • Captions in the chamber on a screen beside the dais, large and high-contrast, with a QR code.
  • Captions on their phone from that QR code, in the language they read, with the text size they need. No app, no account.
  • Captions in the livestream through an overlay in OBS or vMix.
  • The room's own sound on their phone for people with hearing aids or earbuds, when you turn it on.
  • A transcript of every meeting to read, search and download, and a box to ask the whole archive a question ("when did the council discuss the bike lanes?"), answered with the meeting and the moment.

What the clerk's office gets

  • Software you run on your own computer or server, open source, with the transcripts kept there.
  • A glossary for street names, council members and local terms, so they're spelled right.
  • Exports in SRT, VTT and TXT for the meeting's video page and records.
  • Sign-in for staff, two-factor codes and company sign-in; the public pages need none.

What it costs

The software is free. With Google's Gemini in the cloud, about US$ 0.54 for each hour a room is speaking, plus US$ 0.40–0.60 per extra language; nothing is billed while the room is silent. On a Mac with macOS 26, it can run with Apple's own speech recognition at no cost per hour, and no audio leaves the building. See how to caption an event for the setup.
