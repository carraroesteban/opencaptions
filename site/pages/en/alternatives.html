The options

Human captioners (CART)

A trained stenographer writes everything that's said, live, often remotely. It's the most accurate option and the standard for formal proceedings. It's also the most expensive, typically US$90–300 per room-hour for a single language, and it has to be booked ahead.

Simultaneous interpreters

Interpreters translate speech into another spoken language, through headsets or an app. It's the best experience for people listening in another language, but it means interpreters for every language pair and equipment for every room. It doesn't produce captions on its own.

AI captioning and translation services

Commercial platforms such as Wordly or Interprefy sell live AI captions and translation as a hosted service, usually priced by plan or by usage. They're convenient and come with support. Check their current pricing, and where they process your audio, for your event.

Captions in meeting tools

Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet can caption their own calls. They work well for online meetings, but they're built for people in the call, not for an audience sitting in a room reading on their own phones.

Where OpenCaptions fits

OpenCaptions is free, open-source software that you run yourself. It captions and translates every room of an event with AI, and shows the captions on attendees' phones, the stage screen and the livestream.

When to choose something else

  • Every word must be exact, as in courts, hearings or medical settings: book a human captioner, or have someone review the AI transcripts.
  • Your audience needs sign language: captions don't replace sign language interpreters.
  • Nobody on your team can set up a computer with the sound desk and you'd rather have a vendor handle everything: a managed service may be worth its price.

For everything else (conferences, classes, public meetings, festivals, places of worship), OpenCaptions gives every attendee captions in their language for the cost of the AI, or for free.
