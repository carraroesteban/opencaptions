Our target

We aim for WCAG 2.2 level AA on every page of OpenCaptions (the captions pages, the stage screen, the dashboard and the setup wizard) and on this website.

How we check

  • Automatically, on every change. Every page of the software and of this website is opened in Chrome, in light and dark themes and in Spanish and English, and tested with axe-core against WCAG 2.2 AA, and a failure stops the build. All pages pass today.
  • By hand: the documentation has a checklist for keyboard-only use, screen readers (VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA), 200% zoom and reading the stage screen from the back row. We recommend running it before every event.

Automated tests find only part of the problems. The most useful checks are the ones done by people who use assistive technology every day, and we welcome them.

What works

  • Screen readers read each caption once, when the sentence is complete, instead of every word as the line is rewritten.
  • Reading settings on phones: text size, line spacing, high-legibility fonts (Atkinson Hyperlegible, Lexend), light and dark themes, and captions floating over other apps.
  • Keyboard: every control is a real button, link or field, focus is always visible, and dialogs keep focus inside and close with Esc.
  • Colour is never the only signal: statuses and alerts always have words too. Text meets the 4.5:1 contrast ratio in both themes.
  • Motion stops when your system asks for reduced motion, including this website's animations and videos.

Known limits

  • The stream overlay and the stage screen are visual by design. People who use a screen reader follow the same captions on their phone; the stage screen shows the QR code for it.
  • Captions are written by AI. They're fast, but not as accurate as a professional captioner, especially with strong accents, names and people talking over each other.
  • Sign language isn't produced. Captions don't replace interpreters for people whose first language is a sign language.
  • The dashboard and the setup wizard have been tested less with screen readers than the pages the audience uses.

Report a problem

If something in OpenCaptions or on this website is hard or impossible to use, please open an issue on GitHub with the label "accessibility". We treat accessibility problems as bugs. If you're at an event, also tell its organizers: they can switch fonts, sizes and colours on the spot.

The technical details are in the project's accessibility documentation.
