Features

Everything an inclusive event needs.

Accessible for every attendee, effortless for the crew on the day, and yours to keep.

For your audience

  • Captions on any phoneScan the room’s QR code and pick a language. No app, no sign-up.
  • Smooth to readWords appear at the speaker’s pace and lines roll up like TV captions: nothing jumps or re-wraps.
  • Listen in any languageThe translation read aloud: the AI’s natural voice, or the phone’s own voice for any other of 82 languages.
  • What did I miss?A summary of the last five minutes, or the whole session, in their language.
  • Ask the talkQuestions answered only from what was said, with quotes and timestamps.
  • TranscriptsRead, search, print and download every session.
  • Built for accessibilityText size, high-legibility fonts, spacing, light and dark mode, floating captions. Every page is checked against WCAG 2.2 AA.

For the stage and the stream

  • Presenter screenFull-screen captions and the room’s QR code, right next to the speaker.
  • Livestream overlayTransparent or chroma key for OBS and vMix, in one or two languages.
  • Breaks and musicA break in the agenda, a break scene in vMix or OBS, or one key in the room pauses the captions, and screens say when they’re back. Music between talks isn’t captioned.
  • Multilingual speakersWhen a host switches language mid-sentence, every caption language follows.
  • Who’s speakingOne tap names the speaker, the host or the Q&A: captions, transcripts and subtitle files say who said what.
  • Any audio sourceThe sound desk through a PC, a browser tab, or a stream from OBS or vMix.
  • QR postersA printable poster for every room, in one click.
  • Zoom, YouTube Live and TeamsCaptions inside the meeting or the stream, in any language. Webhooks for everything else.

For organizers

  • One dashboard for every roomStatus, audio level, delay, audience, cost and alerts at a glance.
  • Agenda importPaste your Swapcard, Sessionize or spreadsheet export: titles appear automatically.
  • Alerts on your phoneA room loses its sound, a talk runs over, the internet drops: you hear about it wherever you are.
  • Event reportEvery talk with its length, words, audience and cost. Print it for sponsors or open it in a spreadsheet.
  • Safe on event dayEvent mode locks the setup while you’re live, and every change can be undone.
  • Backup audioA second computer on another output of the sound desk takes over by itself if the main one stops or goes silent.
  • Fix a word liveA misheard name is fixed on every screen and phone at once, and the glossary learns it for the rest of the event.
  • Secure by defaultSign-ins that expire, a crew role for volunteers, two-factor codes or your company account, and no open ports.

How it works

Live in three steps.

Connect the audio

Plug in the sound desk through any PC, open a browser tab, or point OBS or vMix at the server.

AI listens and translates

A speech model transcribes every room and translates each sentence with context and your glossary.

Everyone follows along

Phones, the presenter screen and the livestream update together. Transcripts stay online afterwards.

Made for event day

Easy to set up. Hard to knock over.

A guided setup, not a manual

The first time you open the dashboard, a short illustrated wizard asks the essentials and does the rest.

  • Name your event, add your rooms, pick your languages
  • Paste your AI key and get a public address for phones, in one click each
  • Skip anything you’re not sure about and change it later
  • Ends with your QR posters ready to print

Keeps going when the Wi-Fi doesn’t

Use the cloud for its quality, with a laptop at the venue as the backup. If the internet drops, captions keep running on the laptop.

  • Switches by itself in about 15 seconds
  • Goes back to the cloud once the connection is stable
  • A clear banner and a manual switch on the dashboard

You hear about problems first

When nobody is watching the dashboard, OpenCaptions messages your phone: through the free ntfy app, Telegram, Slack, Discord or a webhook.

  • A room loses its sound, or its microphone goes quiet
  • A talk runs past the agenda
  • The internet or the public address drops, and when it’s back

Q&A that’s easy to follow

One tap on the dashboard says who’s talking: the speaker, the host or someone in the audience.

  • Phones show the name when the voice changes
  • Transcripts start a new paragraph for each person
  • Subtitle files carry standard speaker tags

A report for after the applause

Every talk with its length, words, languages, peak audience and AI cost, per room and for the whole event.

  • Print it or save it as a PDF for sponsors
  • Download a spreadsheet that opens in Excel
  • One day or the whole event

The screens

One talk. Every screen in the building.

Also just for you

Your calls, your videos, your language.

Captions on your own computer

Not running an event? Choose Just for me the first time you open OpenCaptions. It captions whatever you’re listening to, and translates it into your language.

  • Video calls, classes and videos: the computer’s sound (on a Mac, a browser tab)
  • The conversation around you, from the microphone, or both sides of a call at once
  • Big text, or a small window that floats over everything
  • Private: nothing is shared, not even on your Wi-Fi

Get started

Up and running in a few minutes.

No terminal needed. Install Node.js, download OpenCaptions for your computer and open it. The setup wizard asks about your event, connects the AI and creates a public address for the QR codes.

Download for Mac Download for Windows Get Node.js

The first time, your Mac may say Apple “could not verify” OpenCaptions, and Windows that it “protected your PC”: the app isn’t signed by Apple or Microsoft yet. Here’s how to open it anyway (once). Linux: use Docker or the commands below.

# Or with Docker, nothing to download first
$ docker run -d --name opencaptions -p 127.0.0.1:8080:8080 -v opencaptions-data:/app/data ghcr.io/carraroesteban/opencaptions
$ docker logs opencaptions | grep "Open the dashboard"

# Developers: clone and start in demo mode
$ git clone https://github.com/carraroesteban/opencaptions.git
$ cd opencaptions && npm install
$ npm run mock            # http://localhost:8080

# In another terminal: play a sample talk into a room
$ npm run feed -- --stage main --input samples/talk-en.wav

# Ready for a real event? Set it up in a minute
$ npm run setup
