Questions

Before you try it.

All the questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The software is free and open source under the MIT license, with no paid plan and no limit on rooms or attendees. If you use Google’s Gemini, Google bills you for it on your own API key: about US$ 0.54 per room-hour of speech. On your own computer, it costs nothing.

Do attendees need an app or an account?

No. They scan the room’s QR code and read the captions in their phone’s browser, in their language. No app, no sign-up, no cookies.

How accurate and how fast is it?

Captions usually appear two to three seconds after the words are spoken, and translations three to five seconds after. It’s good enough for most talks, classes and meetings, but not as accurate as a professional human captioner: add names and specialist terms to the glossary, and the crew can fix a word live.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. The AI can run on a laptop at the venue, free, with phones on the local Wi-Fi: about one room per laptop, a few seconds further behind. Or use the cloud and keep the laptop as a backup that takes over by itself in about 15 seconds.

Where does the audio go?

On your own computer, nowhere: no audio leaves it. With Gemini, the room’s audio goes to Google to be transcribed. Transcripts stay on your computer or server, and audio is never recorded. Privacy
