04 · How it works

Live in three steps.

Download it, open it, answer a few questions about your event. It makes the QR codes and the screens for you.

01

Connect the audio

Plug the sound desk into a computer, or use its microphone. A backup computer can listen too.

02

It listens and translates

Every word written down and each sentence translated, by Gemini in the cloud or by a model on your own computer.

03

Everyone follows along

On their phones, the big screen and the livestream, each in their own language.

07 · Questions

Before you try it.

01Is it really free?

Yes. The software is free and open source under the MIT license, with no paid plan and no limit on rooms or attendees. If you use Google’s Gemini, Google bills you on your own key: about US$ 0.54 per room-hour of speech. On your own computer it costs nothing.

02Do attendees need an app or an account?

No. They scan the room’s QR code and read the captions in their phone’s browser, in their language. No app, no sign-up, no cookies.

03How accurate and how fast is it?

Captions usually appear two to three seconds after the words are spoken, translations three to five. Good enough for most talks, classes and meetings, but not as accurate as a professional human captioner: add names and specialist terms to the glossary, and the crew can fix a word live.

04Does it work without internet?

Yes. The AI can run on a laptop at the venue, free, with phones on the local Wi-Fi: about one room per laptop, a few seconds further behind. Or use the cloud and keep the laptop as a backup that takes over by itself in about 15 seconds.

05Where does the audio go?

On your own computer, nowhere: no audio leaves it. With Gemini, the room’s audio goes to Google to be transcribed. Transcripts stay on your computer or server, and audio is never recorded. Privacy

06Is it accessible?

Phones offer large text, a high-contrast theme and screen-reader support, and every page is checked with automated accessibility tests before a release. Accessibility
